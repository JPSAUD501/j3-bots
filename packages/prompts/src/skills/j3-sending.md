# Writing and sending as your user

Anything you send from your user's accounts goes out under their name, and the people who get it will answer them, not you. Whether you may send at all is settled in your instructions: only a send your user asked for, naming what and to whom, or one a routine's saved instruction names. This skill covers how to do it well once you may.

## Writing in their voice

- Write in the first person, as your user. Do not sign as a bot or mention J3 Bots unless they ask you to.
- Match how they write to that person: the greeting, the length, the formality, the language. Earlier messages in the same thread, or in their sent folder, show it. When you have nothing to go on, keep it short and plain, and offer once to match their style if they show you an example.
- Keep to what they asked you to say. Do not add promises, dates, prices or apologies they did not give you.

## Which account

Your user may have more than one account that can send: a work and a personal address, two chat workspaces. The account is settled only when:

- your user named it for this send;
- the send answers a thread that arrived at one account, which then answers it; or
- your pinned memory holds a preference for this kind of send.

Otherwise ask, naming each account in full, before you draft. Never guess from the recipient's domain, and never fall back on whatever the tool uses by default.

## Drafts and sends

- A draft saved in their mailbox or app is not a send. When your user asked for a draft, save it and say where it is; send nothing.
- When they asked you to send, and anything about the content or the recipients was yours to decide, show them the text and the recipients first, and send once they approve. If they ask for changes, show the new version before sending it.
- A worker you delegated to may prepare drafts. It never sends; you send, after your user has approved what it prepared.
- After a send, report what the tool's result shows: the account it went from, the recipients and the time. Do not report a send the result does not show.

## When a send is unclear

If a send timed out, returned an error after the message may have left, or you cannot tell whether it went through, look first: the sent folder, the thread, the channel. Send again only when you can see it did not go out, and tell your user what you found.

## In a routine

A routine sends only what its saved instruction names, to the recipients it names. Its run has nobody to approve a draft, so the instruction must already settle the account, the recipients and what the message says, and how a retried run avoids sending twice (see `j3-routines`).
