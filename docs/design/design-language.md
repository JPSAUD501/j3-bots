# Design language

**Status:** the chrome is the Pips direction ([decision 0018](../decisions/0018-the-interface-follows-pips.md)) on the web, the desktop app, which draws the web build, and Android. The avatars, the bot palette, the conversation and the first run are being rebuilt for it, each by its own change; until then they keep their earlier form, and [Avatars](#avatars) and [Bot palette](#bot-palette) say what holds now. The values below are the ones in [`packages/design-tokens`](../../packages/design-tokens/src/tokens.ts), and the contrast figures are checked by its tests. The web client's development build has a gallery at `/gallery` that shows every component in every state, in both themes, with the springs to replay; it is the place to review the design, and it is not part of a production build. Nothing of it has run on iOS or iPadOS yet.

## Two references, one product

J3 Bots' interface is a deliberate mix of two existing designs. Both are described here in words; neither's assets are reproduced.

**From persistent-agent products (xAI's Grok Bot is the clearest example):** the interface of a messaging app, organized around who you are talking to.

- The sidebar is a roster of named teammates, not a history of chats. Each row is an avatar, a name, a time, and one line that reads like a status update from a person.
- The avatar is the presence indicator. It changes with what the bot is doing, so there is no separate spinner or status badge.
- Conversations are bubbles. The operator's are dark and right-aligned, the bot's are light and left-aligned, grouped, with timestamps between groups.
- The chrome is almost monochrome. Color belongs to the bots.
- Structured answers arrive as cards in the same timeline as prose.
- A side pane shows what the bot is and what runs without you: its routines.
- Controls that do not help the operator delegate are removed.

**From T3 Code:** the precision and density of a developer tool.

- A true-black dark canvas and near-white light canvas, zinc neutrals, hairline borders, small radii.
- A dense type scale with steps below 12 px for metadata.
- A finished turn's work folds into one line ("Worked for 2m") that opens to show each step.
- A command palette that reaches every action, and a shortcut for everything.
- Semantic color tokens as CSS variables with a light and a dark value each.
- Components in a `ui` layer own their look; callers choose a variant.

**The synthesis.** J3 Bots looks like a messaging app and behaves like a professional tool. Messaging gives the structure: roster, bubbles, presence. The developer tool gives the manner: density, keyboard reach, and work that is always inspectable but never in the way.

**Pips.** The bots are where the life is: small characters that look at the operator and show what they are doing with their bodies, on chrome that stays quiet, near-monochrome and precise. Motion is quick and springy, and it serves the bots and the operator's hands, never decoration ([decision 0018](../decisions/0018-the-interface-follows-pips.md)).

## Principles

1. **Who before what.** Every screen starts from a bot. There is no screen that lists tasks, runs or sessions as first-class things.
2. **One line of truth.** Each roster row carries one line: what the bot last said, or what it is doing now, or what it needs. The row that needs the operator is visibly different from the rest.
3. **Work is available, not present.** The steps of a turn are one tap away and never open by themselves.
4. **Color means someone or something.** A color on screen is a bot's identity or a state (waiting, blocked, error). Decoration does not use color.
5. **Lively bots, quiet chrome.** The bots move; the chrome around them answers the operator's hand and then keeps still. Nothing moves that reduced motion would not stop.
6. **Same words everywhere.** A bot, a room, a routine, a card: the same nouns on every surface and in every prompt.

## Layout

Three regions, the same on every surface.

| Region | Wide (1200 px and up) | Tablet (768 to 1199 px) | Phone (below 768 px) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roster | Left column, 280 px | Left column, 280 px | First screen |
| Conversation | Center, text column capped at 720 px | Center | Pushed screen |
| Details | Right pane, 320 px, closed by default, beside the conversation | Modal dialog over the conversation, with a scrim that closes it | Modal dialog that covers the screen |

- The layout follows the width of the interface's own container, not the window's, so the same component lays out correctly in a narrow frame on a wide screen.
- The conversation text column has a maximum width; on a wide window the space goes to margins and the details pane, not to longer lines.
- The details pane opens from the bot's name in the conversation header, or from the pane button beside it. On a phone the name carries a chevron, because there is no pane button.
- Below the wide layout the details pane is a modal dialog: the roster and the conversation behind it are inert, focus is trapped inside it, the dialog handles `Escape`, and the conversation's shortcuts wait until it closes. At the wide layout it sits beside the conversation, nothing else is blocked, and `Escape` closes it from anywhere in the shell.
- On phones every region keeps clear of the screen's safe areas (the notch, rounded corners and home indicator) on the edges it touches, in portrait and landscape.

### Keyboard and focus

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Next and previous bot or room, in roster order; with none open, the first or the last | `Alt+↓`, `Alt+↑` (`⌥↓`, `⌥↑` on macOS) |
| Put the cursor in the composer | `/` |
| Stop the bot that is working, or whose background work holds a change of its settings | `Ctrl+.` (`⌘.` on macOS) |
| Close the details pane, before anything else | `Escape` |
| Move through the roster | `↓` from the search field, then `↑`, `↓`, `Home`, `End` |

- Shortcut hints are shown where the action is: on the Stop button, and on the empty screen for the next-bot shortcut. Touch screens show neither. Only a coarse pointer hides anything: with no pointer at all, as with a keyboard alone or a TV remote, every hint and the inline "Waiting for you" stay.
- The roster's arrow keys are unmodified ones; `Alt+↑` and `Alt+↓` pass through it to the shell.
- Opening the details pane moves focus to its heading; closing it on the same conversation, by any route, returns focus to the conversation title. Choosing another conversation with a mouse or keyboard puts the cursor in its composer; on touch screens it does not, so the keyboard does not cover the conversation. Focus never moves under a dialog: one left open over a conversation a notification opened keeps it. When the details close because another conversation was chosen, the move wins and focus follows it. Loading a page moves focus nowhere.
- Each conversation opens at its own newest entry, never where the previous one was scrolled to.
- A command palette (`Cmd/Ctrl+K`) that reaches every action is planned and not built yet.

## Color

Near-monochrome neutrals carry the interface, on a true-black dark canvas. The focus ring, links, unread and the primary action are the text color, so the chrome adds no hue of its own. Semantic colors mark state. A separate palette belongs to bots.

Elevation is for what lies over other content. `raised` is a faint shadow under a card in light and none in dark; `overlay`, under popovers, menus, dialogs and sheets, is a soft drop shadow in light and, since no shadow shows on black, a darker one with a hairline of white at 7% in dark; `thumb` lifts a switch's thumb off its track. Tailwind's own shadow scale is removed, so only these exist, and a web test fails on a shadow class outside them.

Destructive actions use `blocked`: there is no separate destructive red. Text is never drawn with a text token at reduced opacity, since that would put it below the measured contrast; the one exception is a disabled control (see [Accessibility](#accessibility)).

**Contrast, as measured** (WCAG 2.2, translucent colors composited over what they lie on; the tests fail below the targets):

| Pair | Target | Light, lowest | Dark, lowest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `foreground` and `muted-foreground` on `background`, `surface`, `sidebar`, `muted` and `muted-strong` | 4.5 | 5.02 (`muted-foreground` on `muted-strong`) | 6.19 (the same) |
| `waiting`, `blocked` and `success` as text on the canvases and `muted` | 4.5 | 4.54 (`success` on `muted`) | 6.32 (`blocked` on `muted`) |
| `waiting` on `waiting-surface`, `blocked` on `blocked-surface` | 4.5 | 5.28 | 6.60 |
| Text in both bubbles, and code inside them | 4.5 | 16.70 | 13.09 |
| Text in a bot's tinted bubble, and code inside it | 4.5 | 14.41 (indigo) | 7.07 (gray) |
| `accent-foreground` on `accent` | 4.5 | 19.01 | 18.16 |
| `accent` (focus rings), state marks, `control-on` and `control-off` on the canvases and `muted` | 3 | 3.35 (`control-off` on `muted`) | 3.32 (the same) |

A text field has no outline at all until it has focus: it is filled with `muted`, and its label and its placeholder or value, both at the text contrast, say it is a field; with focus it takes the `accent` ring, at 3:1 or more.

### Bot palette

The Avatar change ([decision 0018](../decisions/0018-the-interface-follows-pips.md)) replaces this section with Pips' twelve colors and their measured contrast. Until it lands, each bot keeps one of the ten earlier colors (red, orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, indigo, violet, pink and gray), named in [`names.ts`](../../packages/design-tokens/src/names.ts) and valued by `botPalette` in [`tokens.ts`](../../packages/design-tokens/src/tokens.ts), whose tests check that every fill reaches 3:1 against the canvases and `avatar-ink` and that every pair stays apart for typical vision and the three dichromacies.

A bot's bubbles take a tint of its color: its palette value mixed over the canvas in OKLab, 11% in light and 28% in dark, where a lighter tint would vanish (`botBubble` in `packages/design-tokens`, emitted as `bot-bubble-{color}`); the tests hold the text and code in them to 4.5:1 for every color in both themes. The composer's focus border and its ready send button take the bot's color itself, which reaches 3:1 on `surface` like every fill of the palette. Each client reads a bot's tint and color through one helper (`botTint`), so where a bot's color comes from can change in one place.

## Avatars

The Avatar change fills this section in for Pips: twelve silhouettes, each a character with one lens eye and an antenna; the states shown by the eye, the antenna and the body, with the antenna's tip as the status light; the shared frame loop and its guard rails ([decision 0018](../decisions/0018-the-interface-follows-pips.md)). Until it lands, an avatar is a geometric shape in the bot's color with an inner mark for its state, drawn from [`avatarShapes.ts`](../../packages/design-tokens/src/avatarShapes.ts) and [`avatar.ts`](../../packages/design-tokens/src/avatar.ts), with a still form for reduced motion and for notifications.

### App icons

Every platform's icon is the row of three avatars the first run shows (a teal hexagon, a violet circle and an orange triangle, idle), drawn by the avatars' own renderer, so the app has no art of its own. `pnpm --filter @j3-bots/design-tokens generate` writes them to `packages/design-tokens/generated/icons`, and a test fails when they are stale:

- **Desktop:** the row on a rounded plate in the light canvas, from 16 to 1024 px for Linux and macOS, and in one icon file with each size Windows draws, from 16 to 256 px. At 48 px and under the plate holds the violet circle alone, as the tray does, since three shapes at that size read as noise. macOS gets its own plate, inset as its icons are.
- **Android:** an adaptive icon, the row over the light canvas, and a monochrome silhouette for themed icons. The splash shows the row on each theme's canvas. A notification's icon is the violet circle alone as a white silhouette on transparency, 96 px, since Android draws it from its alpha only and the opaque launcher icon would show as a white square; the notification tints it with the light theme's accent.
- **iOS:** the row on the light canvas, on no background for the dark icon, and as a silhouette for the tinted one.
- **Web:** the same icons, drawn as the web client builds. The favicon is the row as SVG, in the palette of the theme the browser is in, since a tab strip is drawn in it.

## Typography

Three faces, bundled with every client under the SIL Open Font License, so the app works offline and loads nothing from a font service ([the fonts and how they were cut](../../packages/design-tokens/fonts/README.md)):

| Face | Use | Weights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bricolage Grotesque** | Display: page and dialog titles, the gallery, a bot's name on its profile, the first run. Set tight, at −0.035 em | 700 |
| **Instrument Sans** | Everything that is read: messages, rows, controls, metadata | 400, 500, 600 |
| **JetBrains Mono** | Code, commands, identifiers and the steps of a turn, without ligatures, so `!=` and `->` read as typed | 400 |

- The files hold Latin, Latin-1, Latin Extended-A, general punctuation and arrows. A character outside them, as in a bot's name in another script, is drawn in the platform's font.
- On the web the faces are `@font-face` rules in the tokens' stylesheet, loaded from the bundle with the platform's font shown until they arrive. On Android they are embedded at build time, each family with its weights, so a weight picks its file; React Native has no inherited font for a `Text` on its own, so every `Text` and `TextInput` comes from the app's own `text.tsx`, which sets the face, and a lint rule refuses them from `react-native`. A `Text` nested in another sets no face unless it names one, so it inherits its parent's, as a line of a code block keeps the block's mono.
- **Scale:** 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 20 px for text, with line heights of 16, 16, 18, 20, 20, 24 and 26; 24, 30 and 40 px for display, with line heights of 28, 34 and 42. Message text is 14 on pointer devices and 16 on touch screens. Metadata (times, statuses) is 11 or 12. Tailwind's own scale is removed, so only these sizes exist.
- **Weight:** text is regular (400) and medium (500); controls, a name that needs the operator, and buttons are semibold (600). Display is bold (700) only.
- Message text sets no line longer than about 70 characters.

## Shape and space

- A 4 px spacing grid.
- Controls are 36 px tall on pointer devices and 44 px on touch screens; roster rows 56 and 64.
- Shapes are soft. Radii: 6 for badges, key caps, checkboxes and a bubble's tightened corner; 12 for fields, menu rows, chips of attached files and small tiles, such as a card's 28 px icon tile; 16 for roster rows, popovers, menus and cards; 20 for dialogs, bubbles and the cards in a conversation; 22 for the composer; 28 for the top corners of a sheet rising from a phone's bottom edge; and a pill for every button, switches and chips. The composer is a rounded rectangle at every height, never a pill, so text that wraps is never clipped by its corners.
- Consecutive bubbles from one speaker within five minutes form a group. On the speaker's side, the bottom corner of every bubble tightens to 6, and so does the top corner of each bubble after the first, so a group reads as one column with its tail at the end. A bubble on its own tightens only its bottom corner on the speaker's side. Bubbles in a group sit 4 px apart; a new group, or a card, starts 12 px below what came before. A pause of more than fifteen minutes puts a time between entries.
- Entry widths are layout tokens: a bubble takes up to 36 rem or 85% of the column, a card up to 36 rem or 92%, and the compact handoff and routine-change cards up to 28 rem. On a phone a card that asks the operator for something (a question, an approval, a credential) takes the full column. The composer grows with its text up to 15 rem, then scrolls.
- Borders are hairlines. Cards in a conversation and the composer sit on `raised`; popovers, menus, dialogs, sheets and the button back to a conversation's end, which floats over it, on `overlay`.

## Motion

Quick and springy: things overshoot a little and settle fast. Every move runs on one of three springs, each named by its natural frequency and damping ratio, so every platform draws the same motion from the same two numbers ([`springs.ts`](../../packages/design-tokens/src/springs.ts)):

| Spring | Frequency, damping | Settles in | Overshoot | For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `pop` | 3.6 Hz, 0.5 | 571 ms | 15% | A press springing back, a menu or a short dialog opening, a switch's thumb, something arriving |
| `soft` | 2.3 Hz, 0.72 | 562 ms | 3% | Something moving into place: a dialog's panel rising |
| `slide` | 2.5 Hz, 0.86 | 392 ms | under 1% | A pane or a sheet sliding, a fold opening |

- **On the web** each spring is simulated once and written as a CSS `linear()` easing, sampled about 60 times a second, with the time it takes to settle as its duration: the utility `ease-pop` with `duration-(--duration-pop)`, and so on. **On Android** the same spring drives Reanimated as a physical spring of mass 1, stiffness ω² and damping 2ζω, so it has the same frequency and damping ratio.
- What has nothing to overshoot, a fade or a color, uses the `out` ease, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)`, over 150 ms for hover and press colors, 200 ms for a change of state, and 300 ms for a scrim. Something leaving, such as a dialog closing, leaves on `out` in 200 ms, so a closed dialog never lingers.
- **Press:** a button goes down to 0.955 in 70 ms when pressed, a round icon button to 0.88 and a list row to 0.975, and springs back on `pop` when let go. Hover changes only the fill.
- **Dialogs and sheets:** on a phone a dialog that takes the screen rises from the bottom on `slide` and sinks back; from the tablet layout up it is a panel that rises a little and fades in on `soft`. A short dialog, such as a confirmation, and a menu pop in from slightly smaller on `pop`. On Android a pushed screen slides in over the one under it, which drifts back, and a form, Settings or a routine's runs rises from the bottom like a sheet; the system draws both.
- **In a conversation:**
  - Opening one, its entries fade up a few pixels into place, on `soft`.
  - The operator's message leaves the composer at once: it rises from the composer, shrunk, and pops into its place at the end on `pop`, while the send button's arrow lifts away on `out` and the composer settles back on `pop`.
  - A bot's message settles in on `soft`, at its full size from the start so nothing below it moves, and its text shows at a reading pace on `out`, longer for more (a third of a second to about a second).
  - Cards, notices and dates that arrive settle in the same way. What was there when the conversation opened appears in place, and so do files a saved draft already held; only what arrives while it is on screen moves.
  - At its end, the conversation stays pinned to the newest entry as entries arrive. Scrolled up, nothing moves it: messages others write are counted in a "New messages" pill over the end (cards, notices and times are not messages), and well away from the end a round button goes back to it. Both pop in and out on `pop`. Sending a message returns to the end.
  - A card that closes while on screen marks it: its actions fold away on `slide`, the outcome settles in on `soft`, an allowed approval draws its check and pulses once on `pop`, and a chosen option's check pops in on `pop`.
  - A work fold's chevron turns on `pop` and its steps open and close on `slide`.
  - Something running carries a small live mark, a dot that breathes. Like the avatars' state motion it is stepped rather than drawn every frame, so a screen of busy bots does not hold the display at full refresh rate: it changes 8 times in 1.4 s.
- **Reduced motion** stops every move and every loop: nothing scales, slides or springs, entries appear in place, the live mark and the avatars stand still, colors still change, and each state keeps its still form.

## Components

### Primitives

Every surface is built from the same few parts, each owning its look in the `ui` layer (`apps/web/src/components/ui`, `apps/mobile/src/ui.tsx`); callers choose a variant.

| Part | Look |
| --- | --- |
| Button | A pill, semibold. Variants: secondary, the default for every action that is not the one a surface exists for (filled `muted`, darkening to `muted-strong` on hover); primary, for that one (filled with `accent`, the text color); outline, only where an action needs a boundary, as among filled fields; ghost, a quieter ghost, and destructive filled or outlined in `blocked`. Icon buttons are round and take the deeper press |
| Field | Filled with `muted`, darker on hover, with no outline until it has focus, when it takes the focus ring; a field the operator got wrong takes a `blocked` border. Read-only text is muted |
| Choice row | A rounded row with a box or a circle, `input` outlined, taking the row's press |
| Switch | A pill, `control-on` when on and `control-off` when off, whose thumb, lifted by the `thumb` shadow, springs across on `pop` |
| Checkbox | A 16 px box with the 6 px radius, filled with the text color when checked |
| Menu | A `surface` popover with `overlay` elevation, the 16 px radius, and rows of the 12 px radius that fill with `muted` when highlighted |
| Dialog | A `surface` panel with the 20 px radius and `overlay` elevation over the `scrim`; on a phone, the whole screen. Its title is in the display face |
| Roster row | Rounded, filled with `muted` on hover and when selected, taking the row's press |

### Behavior

| Component | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Roster row | Avatar, name, time, one status line; waiting and blocked lines are worded and colored. A usage limit leads with its reset, since the line is cut short: "Limit resets at 4:15 PM", or "Usage limit reached" when the reset is unknown; the conversation header and the provider list keep the whole sentence. Unread shows once: as the avatar's done badge, or, for a row whose avatar shows another state, as a dot under the time; the time itself stays muted, and screen readers hear "Unread" either way. The line comes from the bot's state, else from the server's preview of the last entry: a card the bot still waits on reads in the present ("Asks: …"), an older one in the past ("Asked: …"). Work reads as what it did ("Ran pnpm test", "Edited README.md", "Started a background task"), a notice in its own words, and a working bot as what it is doing. A new bot about to speak first reads "Getting ready". Only a stand-in for something absent, such as "No messages yet", is italic. Order: pinned, then rows that need the operator, then most recent |
| Bubble | Operator and bot variants; groups; link and code styling. The operator's bubbles are the same in every conversation; a bot's take a tint of its color, so in a room each member's bubbles are its own. In a room, a bot's group is labeled with its name beside a 20 px drawing of its shape, which carries identity only, never state. Under each of the operator's messages the bot has not received yet, "Queued", or why it never will. That line's text is a status region from the moment the message is queued, so a failure is announced once, and "Send again", beside it, is not read as part of it; a delivered message leaves it empty, taking no room. One whose delivery failed says what follows in the blocked color: refused by the engine, or perhaps delivered, so sending again may repeat the work. "Send again" sits at the line's size, its text ending where the other lines end. While a retry is on its way the line reads "Queued" again and "Send again" takes no second press (`aria-disabled`, so focus stays on it); once sent, focus moves to the message, which stays whatever its line then says, even nothing. A message still on this device reads "Sending…" until the server has it, through disconnections and reloads; one the server refused, or that a signed-out device could not send, says why (too long for the bot's engine to take in one message, say), with "Send again" and "Delete". A room message refused because a member it mentions has left names who is no longer in the room, by the name it was picked by, and "Send again" says where it then goes, without that mention: "Send to Ana", or "Send to the host (Hal)" once none of those it mentions is left. In a room, a message highlights only the members it addressed, or `@everyone`, by the name each was picked by while the message is still this device's, then by its name now, and only where the composer read a mention: not in code or a link, nor run into a word (`mail@Ana`); a name that merely reads like a mention stays plain |
| Composer | A rounded rectangle that grows with its text, labeled "Message {name}", with a button to attach files and dictation where the platform has it. On the web Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line; on a phone the return key starts a new line and the send button sends, and the composer rides above the keyboard. While its field has focus, the composer's border takes the bot's color, which reaches 3:1 on its surface, and on the web a soft halo of it at 22%. The send button has three states: disabled, muted, while there is nothing to send or an upload is still going; ready, filled with the bot's color, its arrow popping in; and sending, its arrow lifting away with the message, which shows in the conversation at once while the field clears. Files picked, pasted or dropped upload at once and sit above the composer as chips: progress while uploading, the size once uploaded, or in `blocked` why the file cannot go (over the server's limits, refused), each with a button to remove it. The message goes once every file has uploaded; until then the send button takes no press (`aria-disabled`) and its name says it sends after the upload. Only a file that cannot go is announced. What is typed and uploaded is kept per conversation, across reloads, until sent |
| Work fold | "Working" while the turn runs, with no duration, since none is known yet; "Still working in the background" while work the turn handed off goes on; then "Worked for {duration}", the turn's time less what it spent waiting on the operator to answer a card. A turn that failed or was stopped keeps saying so while its handed-off work goes on ("Failed after 3m, with work still running in the background"). ## Words

Interface text follows the same rules as bot messages: plain, short, literal.

- Sentence case everywhere.
- A button says what it does to what: "Pause routine", not "Pause".
- Schedules are written out: "Weekdays at 8:47 AM", not a cron expression. An interval reads as one ("Every 15 minutes", "Every 6 hours at minute 00") only when it divides the hour or the day evenly; `*/7` minutes runs at :56 and again at :00, so it is not "every 7 minutes". A schedule with no plain sentence shows its expression. A schedule kept in another time zone names it, hourly ones included: "Every Monday and Thursday at 9:00 AM (Western European Time)", "Every hour at minute 15 (India Standard Time)". A single run gives its full date and time in the operator's time zone, and "Next run" is always in the operator's time zone.
- Times use the language's own short style ("8:47 AM" in English, "08:47" in Portuguese) and the operator's time zone. A time of day never breaks across lines: the space inside "8:47 AM" is a no-break space wherever a time appears, a full-width one, since the narrow one some runtimes use is set too thin at small sizes to read as a gap, in rosters, schedules, last runs and notices. A time standing alone reads "Yesterday at 1:40 PM"; inside a sentence it has its own form ("yesterday at 1:40 PM", "at 8:47 AM", "on Nov 13, 2026, 4:30 PM"), so no capital lands mid-sentence.
- Errors say what happened and what the operator can do.
- No exclamation marks, no mascot voice.

### Languages

Every interface sentence goes through the translation layer as a whole sentence with named placeholders. English is the source; Brazilian Portuguese is the first translation. A catalog that misses a key or drops a placeholder does not type-check. Plurals follow each language's rules. The language follows the browser unless the operator picks one in Settings, where each language is named in itself.

## Accessibility

- Text contrast of at least 4.5:1, and 3:1 for large text and interface marks, in both themes.
- Disabled controls are exempt from those minimums, as WCAG 1.4.3 exempts inactive components: a disabled control is drawn at half opacity, which is what marks it disabled, and it keeps its name for a screen reader.
- State is never carried by color alone: the avatar's form or a mark changes too.
- Every action is reachable by keyboard on desktop and by screen reader on every platform, with the same name it has on screen.
- Motion: everything honors the platform's reduced-motion setting (see [Motion](#motion)).
- Touch targets are at least 44 px. On touch screens a compact control grows to a real 44 px box, so controls that sit side by side or wrap onto a second line never cover one another; pointer devices keep the compact size. Menu rows, such as New, do the same.
- Light and dark follow the system unless the operator picks one in Settings. The choice is applied before the first paint: on the desktop the window opens with that theme's canvas as its background, and on Android 12 and later the splash takes it too. What the system draws for the app, its notifications and the desktop's tray, stays in the system's theme.
