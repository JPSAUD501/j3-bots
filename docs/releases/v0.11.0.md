# J3 Bots 0.11.0

**Draft.** The notes for the 0.11.0 release, kept here while it is prepared. The release workflow adds the install section and the Android certificate's digest.

This release shows more of what a bot does while it works, in its own way. Its plan is a quiet card that moves on as it works. When it splits its work, it pops out little copies of itself that work under its face and jump back in when they finish. A failure says what happened and what to do, and Force stop ends a turn that ignores Stop. After a turn, the composer offers the reply you are likely to send. A picture the bot sends arrives as a photo. On the desktop, the shortcut opens quick access to your primary bot. It still pins T3 Code 0.1.11.

## Updating

This release speaks **protocol 11**, as 0.9.0 and 0.10.0 do, so an app and a server on any of them keep talking in either order. Update the server first anyway: the plan, the little ones, Force stop and suggested replies need it.

- **The server:** from an app, in Settings → About and updates, or `j3-bots update` on its machine. Either copies the databases into a snapshot first, then runs this release's migrations:
  - **0053** keeps where each image a bot looked at is, under an id only the server knows.
  - **0054** adds the switch for suggested replies, and records which message a turn said in its conversation.
  - **0055** keeps each turn's plan and little ones.
  - **0056** records a retry in progress, and a Stop the AI ignored, so Force stop can be offered.
- **The desktop app and the Android app** update themselves as they open, or from the prompt every paired app shows. The deb and the Mac build link to this release.
- **The web client** comes with the server.

### Rolling back

`j3-bots update --rollback` to 0.10.0 puts back the databases from the snapshot, so the messages, rooms and changes made since the update are lost with the rest of the database. The apps keep talking to it, since 0.10.0 speaks protocol 11 too. Don't start 0.10.0 by hand on the updated data: it refuses a database with these migrations, and says to roll back.

## What's new

### Quick access, on the desktop

- **The shortcut opens quick access to your primary bot** ([#346](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/346)). From inside any other app, it opens a small card in the bottom-right corner: the bot's live face, its recent conversation and the composer, with the cursor in it. Make a quick request and go back to what you were doing. The tray's Show J3 Bots still opens the main window.
- **It is the same conversation** as in the main window: what you read in one is read in the other, the draft is the same draft, and files dropped or pasted on it are attached. The plan, the little ones, Force stop and suggested replies show in it too.
- **Drag it by its header** wherever you like: it comes back where you last put it on each display. `Escape`, its button or the shortcut again closes it; it never closes on its own when you click elsewhere. **Open conversation** takes you to the main window.
- **It opens at once**: the window is kept ready, hidden. With no primary bot or no server, the shortcut shows the main window instead.
- **A draft typed in one window shows in the others at once**, in browser tabs too, so a message typed in quick access after one sent from the main window is no longer put aside.

### The plan and the little ones

- **The plan is a quiet card** ([#340](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/340), [#345](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/345)). Under the turn's line, one line a step, done steps checked, and one live mark in the bot's color that travels to the step it is on. When the turn ends, the card folds into "Plan done, 4 of 4", which opens every step. The "Updated its plan" step that repeated on every change is gone.
- **When a bot splits its work, it pops out its little ones** ([#340](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/340), [#345](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/345)). It braces, wobbles and pops out small copies of itself, one after another. They fall into a column under its face, each by one line of what it does: four at most, then "+2 more". One that finishes jumps back into the bot, which gulps and glows once. One that failed turns grey for a moment before it goes home. A hover or a press shows its line in full and how long it has worked. Little ones are not helpers: they have no name and no conversation, and helpers stay bots of their own.
- **Everywhere the bot works:** in its conversation under the turn, in a room under the member whose turn it is, in quick access, and in the roster as one dot per little one at work. With "Remove animations" on, nothing travels: little ones appear and go.

### Errors that make sense, and Force stop

- **A failure says what to do** ([#339](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/339)). A failed turn reads as one of a few plain sentences: a usage limit with its reset time, "Claude was signed out. Sign in again in Settings.", "Couldn't reach Claude. Check the internet connection, then try again.", or "Claude ran into a problem and couldn't finish. Try again.". The technical part sits under **Details** and goes to the server's log, without the secrets J3 Bots gave the engine.
- **Trying again is a quiet state.** While the AI is retried, the bot's line says "Having trouble reaching Claude, trying again". When the retry works, nothing is left behind.
- **Force stop.** When the AI ignores Stop, Stop becomes **Force stop**, with one line on why, in a bot's conversation and in rooms. It does what Stop does and also ends the program the AI is running. Files written and commands run stay done, and the conversation carries on in the next turn.
- **Questions arrive whole.** A card shows every question the AI asked, however many, with their headers and option previews, where a larger one used to leave the turn waiting on an answer you could not give.

### Suggested replies

- **After a turn, the composer offers what you are likely to say next** ([#343](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/343)), as T3 Code does. It shows faintly in place of the placeholder, with **Use** beside it (or Tab on the desktop and web). It never sends itself, and typing, sending or a new message hides it.
- **Only in a bot's own conversation, never in a room**, and only after a turn that you started there, so a guess never carries another conversation's content.
- **It is on by default, and costs one small request to your AI account for each finished turn.** Settings → Providers turns it off.

### Images

- **A picture the bot sends arrives as a photo** ([#338](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/338)), after the message's text, and opens large on a tap. The bots are told to attach a picture they made or picked for you, not to give its path.
- **An image the bot only looked at stays in its work.** Open the folded work and that step shows it as a thumbnail, large on a tap. The server serves it to your paired devices by an id it made, only from the bot's workspace, and only if it is really an image.

## Known limits

**Not tried for real yet:**

- **Quick access's see-through window on a real desktop.** CI runs it on Linux without a compositor, where it is solid and square: Windows, macOS, a composited Linux desktop and Wayland were not run.
- **The Android app** didn't run on a phone: the little ones, the plan, Force stop, the whole questions, suggested replies and photos were checked by their tests and types only.
- **Force stop against a real AI.** None of the AIs in the test suite ignores a stop, so it was checked against the scripted engine only.
- **The plan, the little ones and suggested replies against real AIs.** They follow T3 Code's source and engine-shaped fixtures.
- **What a model does with the new picture instructions**: their eval cases are written and were not run.

**Limits of this version:**

- **On Wayland, the system decides where quick access opens** and whether it stays above other windows.

- **A Codex subagent's own subagents are not shown**: the engine keeps them where J3 Bots does not follow. Claude Code's are.
- **A label depends on the AI.** Claude Code describes each subagent; for Codex, a little one's line is the first words of what it was asked.
- **A suggested reply comes from the AI's whole session**, so it can lean on what the bot read while working, which you did not see. It shows only in the bot's own conversation.
- **An image that exists only inside a tool's output**, such as an inline browser screenshot, shows no thumbnail.

Everything listed under "Known limits" in the [0.10.0](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/releases/tag/v0.10.0) notes still applies.
