# J3 Bots 0.15.0

**Draft.** The notes for the 0.15.0 release, kept here while it is prepared. The release workflow adds the install section and the Android certificate's digest.

Each bot has a card that says who it is and what it is doing, and its title sits under its name everywhere. Bots can talk in rooms of their own, which you read. You can set up several AI accounts and pick a bot's model, effort and speed in one picker. Themes change how J3 Bots looks, and a bot can make one for you. Dictation goes through the cloud service you choose, with your own key, and a screen recording now carries what you said. Every new bot can browse the web. Quotes sit in the message field, and nothing sits under it. The words across the apps are shorter and plainer, and the app opens faster. It still pins T3 Code 0.1.13.

## Updating

This release speaks **protocol 11**, as 0.9.0 to 0.14.0 do, so an app and a server on any of them keep talking in either order. Update the server first anyway: everything new needs it. Then update the apps.

An app older than 0.15.0 shows a bot's name without its title, and "Hidden" where an updated app says "Behind the scenes". It has no bot card, no themes beyond its own, no AI accounts to add and no new model picker. It shows a bot's offer of a theme as the message alone, and a quote without its comment. It shows a room of bots only with a message field, and the server refuses what it sends there. On the web and the desktop it cannot dictate against this server, which no longer runs a speech model; the Android app still dictates with the phone's own recognizer.

**Set up dictation after updating.** The server no longer turns speech into text itself. On the web and the desktop, dictation and a recording's transcript need a cloud service and its key: open Settings → Voice, choose OpenAI, xAI or OpenRouter as the **Dictation service**, and paste a key (**Get a key** links to the page that makes one). Until then the microphone opens that page, and a recording goes without a transcript. The key is kept in Secrets.

**The old speech model stays on disk.** A server that dictated on 0.14.0 downloaded a speech model, about 751 MB. 0.15.0 does not use it and does not delete it. To free the space, delete the `speech/models` folder in the engine's state folder ([Operating: dictation](../operations/operating.md#dictation)). A key that a 0.14.0 server handed the engine for a dictation stopped midway may also still be in the engine's secret store; revoke it at the provider if that matters to you.

**Bots made before 0.15.0 keep their browser switch as it was.** Every new bot starts with the browser on. To let an older bot browse, open its details and turn on **Can browse the web** ("Pode navegar na internet" in Portuguese).

- **The server:** from an app, in Settings → About and updates, or `j3-bots update` on its machine. Either copies the databases, the engine's too, into a snapshot first, then runs this release's migrations, with nothing for you to do:
  - **0066** keeps who made a bot, you or another bot, for its card. A bot made before it says only when it was made, unless a bot that still exists made it.
  - **0067** keeps whether a room is for bots only. Every room from before has you in it.
  - **0068** adds indexes, so the work behind each turn reads less and deleting a bot is fast.
  - **0069** keeps the dictation service you choose. A server that dictated on its own now dictates automatically, which on the web and the desktop needs a service and key; a choice that named a service keeps it.
  - **0070** keeps the themes you and your bots saved, and the theme new devices start with, Palco.
  - **0071** keeps your AI accounts. The account every install had stays the first of its kind, so every bot stays where it was. It also keeps each bot's speed, Standard, and the models you star, none at first.
- **The desktop app and the Android app** update themselves as they open, or from the prompt every paired app shows. The deb and the Mac build link to this release.
- **The web client** comes with the server.

### Rolling back

`j3-bots update --rollback` to 0.14.0 puts back the databases from the snapshot, the engine's included, so the themes, AI accounts, rooms, messages and settings changed since the update are lost, the dictation service with them. The apps keep talking to it, since 0.14.0 speaks protocol 11 too. Both releases pin T3 Code 0.1.13, so the engine's data is not moved forward. 0.14.0 refuses a database with these migrations: started by hand on the updated data, the server exits with status 78 and says to run `j3-bots update --rollback`. On 0.14.0 the server dictates as it did before, with its own model, which its first dictation downloads again if you deleted it.

## What's new

### Dictation

- **Dictation goes through the cloud service you choose, with your own key: set it up in Settings → Voice** ([#403](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/403)).
  - On the web and the desktop, what you say goes to OpenAI, xAI or OpenRouter, with your key from Secrets. The server no longer runs a speech model or downloads one. On Android, the phone's own recognizer still writes it down when it knows the language.
  - **The message field stays yours while you talk.** The microphone becomes a small capsule in the field: Cancel, a red dot, the time, your voice's level, and Stop. You can keep typing meanwhile. On Stop it says "Writing it down…", and the words land where the cursor was.
  - **Settings → Voice is one page:** the service, its key, OpenRouter's model, **Test**, and the language. **On your phone** says where a phone dictates. Words to spell right and taking out "um" and "uh" are under More.
  - A server takes several dictations at once.

### A bot's card

- **Rest on a bot's face or name to see its card: who it is, what it is doing, and a quick message** ([#398](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/398)).
  - On the web and the desktop, it opens after the pointer rests on a bot's face or name for half a second, or as the keyboard reaches a bot: in the list, in a room, among teammates and in a mention. It closes when you move away or press Esc. On Android, a long press on a bot's face opens it as a sheet.
  - It shows the bot's face with how it is doing (working, waiting for you, free, paused by a usage limit, or stopped by a problem), its name and title, what it is for, who created it and when, its routines and rooms, and its little ones while it works.
  - **Talk**, **Start a room**, **Routines** and **Details** are one tap away. A quick message goes to the bot's conversation without leaving where you are, and the card says "Sent to Wren." with a link to the conversation.

### AI accounts and the model picker

- **Set up several AI accounts, and pick a bot's model, effort and speed in one place** ([#399](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/399)).
  - **Settings → Providers** lists each Claude Code and Codex account with its status, version and sign-in, and a switch that turns it on or off. **Add account** adds another of the same kind, signed in on its own. **Advanced** holds its name, the program it runs and its launch options. An account you added can be removed; the first of each kind can only be turned off.
  - **One picker** chooses a bot's AI wherever you make or edit a bot, or create one from a template: a rail of accounts and Starred, search across every account, stars for the models you use most, the effort, and **Standard** or **Fast** for a model that has a fast mode. Android has the same picker in a sheet.
  - **A bot whose account is off or removed** keeps its model and waits. Its conversation says so and offers another model. J3 Bots never moves it by itself.

### Themes

- **Change how J3 Bots looks with a theme, or make your own from two colors** ([#401](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/401)).
  - Settings → Appearance has seven themes, each in light and dark: Palco, Graphite, Ocean, Forest, Ember, Lavender and Paper. Each device keeps its own choice. **Start new devices with it** sets what a device shows until someone picks.
  - **Create from colors** makes one from a name, a background and an accent, with light and dark previews as you type. J3 Bots keeps every theme readable: it adjusts a color that would be too faint and says so, or refuses the theme and says why.
  - **A bot can make a theme for you.** It saves the theme and offers it under its message; **Apply theme** puts it on the device you press it on. A bot never applies one by itself.
  - A theme can also change shapes and spacing, which shows only on a computer, and never in Settings. Ctrl+, (⌘, on a Mac) always opens Settings.

### The browser, and plain words

- **Every new bot can browse the web; bots made before 0.15.0 keep their switch as it was** ([#400](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/400)).
  - New bots start with the browser on. To let an older bot browse, turn on **Can browse the web** in its details.
  - The line under the switch says what is true: the bots share one browser, and a bot calls you when a site asks you to sign in. While the bot is working, it says that turning the switch off stops what it is doing.
- **The words across the apps are shorter and plainer** ([#400](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/400)). About a hundred of them, in English and Portuguese. A turn is now a task, a provider an AI account, and the screens shown while J3 Bots starts name J3 Bots rather than its engine. On an approval, "Allow for session" is now **Don't ask again**, which lasts until the bot starts fresh.

### Rooms of bots only

- **Bots can talk in a room of their own, which you read and never write in** ([#397](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/397)).
  - When you ask a bot to get other bots working together, it chooses whether you are in the room. A room of bots only reads like a teammate conversation: in place of the message field it says "Read-only · Only bots talk here", and its messages take no reply, quote or reaction from you.
  - **It pauses on its own** after a few rounds, so it never runs up your AI's allowance. The room says so, and the bot that started it tells you where it got to. To go on, ask for a new room.
  - You can still pin it, move it behind the scenes, archive, stop or delete it.

### Name and title, and Behind the scenes

- **A bot's title sits under its name wherever the bot is named** ([#393](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/393)).
  - In the list, a row shows the name, the title in smaller words, then what the bot is doing. A conversation's header shows the title under the name, and in a room each speaker reads "Scout · Product research". Mentions, member lists, quick access and notifications show both.
  - A new bot needs a title, a few words on why it exists. Bots your bots make get one too. A bot from before titles shows its name alone, and its menu offers **Give a title**.
  - **"Hidden" is now "Behind the scenes".** The last row of the list is **Behind the scenes · 3**, and its actions are **Move behind the scenes** and **Bring back to the list**. A helper another bot makes behind the scenes no longer says hello; it waits for the bot that made it.

### Screen recording

- **A screen recording carries what you said, with times** ([#396](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/396), [#403](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/403)).
  - After Stop, the desktop app writes your voice down before the recording uploads, and its chip says "Writing down what you said" with how far it got. The bot reads what you said beside what you did.
  - **It needs the dictation service and key.** Without them, the recording goes without a transcript, as before, and says so to the bot.
  - The control that floats while you record is now small and in the app's look: the bot's face, a red dot, your voice's level, and Pause, Stop and Discard. Discard asks once more.

### Quotes, and the message field

- **A reply or a quote sits inside the message field, and you can comment on a passage you quote** ([#402](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/402)).
  - Each one is a small chip above your words, with the author's face, the start of the words and a ✕. In a sent message, quotes sit inside its bubble.
  - Quoting a passage opens a small card for an optional comment, which the bot reads with the passage. The chip's pencil edits it later. On Android, Quote part has a comment field.
  - **Nothing sits under the message field any more.** Dictation's state, a failed send, files that couldn't go and the like show in one line at the top of the field.
  - What's new shows in the list only while there is something you haven't seen. It is always in Settings → About and updates.

### Faster and lighter

- **J3 Bots opens faster, and deleting a busy bot takes a second rather than a minute or more** ([#394](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/394)).
  - The web client loads what a window shows first and the rest when it is wanted, only in your language, and the server sends it compressed: about 680 KB over the network where it was about 2.5 MB.
  - During a turn, the server sends a bot's row in the list only when it changed, about a third less traffic.
  - Deleting a bot that worked a lot took over 90 seconds on a large server; it now takes about one.

### Nightly builds

- **A nightly's Android app is signed** ([#395](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/pull/395)). Before this, the nightly's APK was left unsigned, so it could not be installed.

## Known limits

**Not tried for real yet:**

- **The Android app** didn't run on a phone: the bot card's sheet, titles in the list, the read-only room, the quote chips and comments, the model picker, themes repainting and the dictation capsule were checked by tests and types only. The change to the native message field was not compiled (**Assumed** to build).
- **The desktop app** was not built: the recording control, the transcript, quick access opening a bot's details, the windows' theme colors and Go → Settings are unseen. Nothing ran on macOS or Windows.
- **Dictation** never reached a real provider, and no real microphone was used: the tests answer as a provider would.
- **AI accounts** were not added or removed against a running engine; that rests on T3 Code's source (**Read**). No real sign-in was made.
- **Against real AIs**: nothing ran. Whether bots give good titles, choose well whether you are in a room, or offer themes as meant had no real-model eval.
- **Memory on a 4 GB ARM board**: the server's growth there from 0.13.0 to 0.14.0 did not show on a desktop with no app connected, and is unexplained.

**Limits of this version:**

- **Dictation on the web and the desktop** needs a cloud service and its key, and you pay that service for what it writes down. The voice leaves your machines for it. Words appear only once you stop, on Android too when it uses the service.
- **A recording's transcript** is all or nothing: if a later part fails for good, the recording goes without one. On Linux, the floating control shows in the video.
- **AI accounts**: only Claude Code and Codex. `j3-bots provider login` signs in only the first account of each kind; sign an added account in from J3 Bots. When two Codex accounts sign in at the same time, the second stops the first.
- **A room of bots only** pauses after a few rounds and does not resume; going on takes a new room. An app older than 0.15.0 shows it with a message field, and what it sends there fails.
- **Themes** change shapes and spacing only on a computer; phones and the Android app show their colors. The Android home-screen widget keeps its own colors. A bot saves no theme once 50 are saved.
- **A window's part that loads later** shows the app's error screen if its first load fails while offline.

Everything listed under "Known limits" in the [0.14.0](https://github.com/JPSAUD501/j3-bots/releases/tag/v0.14.0) notes still applies, except that a recording now has a transcript and a server dictates several at once.
